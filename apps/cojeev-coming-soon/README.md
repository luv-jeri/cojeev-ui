## The Bond interaction demo

The home page (`index.html`, served at `/`) is the Bond, an interactive story: a single prompt line that a living presence bonds with, and the conversation that grows out of it. Six small organisms roam the whole viewport, curious about the prompt. Enter (or the arrow, or a picked thought) sends them to the line along their own momentum; they clasp its ends, the glass field dissolves as they spread into a membrane, and the prompt lives inside. The line then grows into a conversation panel: the prompt lifts into a sent bubble, the field drops to the bottom as the composer, and the lifecycle plays inside the body as short sentences and reaching tendrils. Scattered over the whole stage floats an environment of titled blobs, sixteen from the start: memories, files, skills, teammates with names, subagents with roles. Each kind has its own body (a small gel with a highlight, an edge in its deep tone and a soft shadow) and its own idle life: memories breathe out a recall ring, files flutter, skills twinkle, a teammate’s presence dot breathes, a small satellite orbits a subagent and speeds up while it works, reports glint; all of them slowly shift shape and drift. The whole composition scales with the viewport (1 at 1440 × 900, up to 1.8× on a large screen; phones keep their own hand-tuned sizes). The What’s coming tray is the library’s sheet, ink in light and paper in dark, floating inset from the edges on the sheet radius (capped at 1240 px and centred on wide screens), carrying five cards with gel glyphs; the story and the field keep moving behind a light veil, and a scroll up or a swipe down closes it. The organism reaches out from whichever edge is nearest and handles each kind its own way: it grabs the memories it needs, absorbs what matters and lets them go (they float on); it draws the story’s file and skill in and holds them close against its body until the story settles; it hands the task to the teammate, whose subagent then informs the other subagents; it types a real three-sentence reply that gets a second look; and it places two new things out into the environment, a memory and a report, which a later thought can grab. At rest three thoughts float under the field as bubbles to pick; after a settle one bubble offers the next thought. A later thought folds the previous exchange into one line at the top rim of the panel (its prompt, what was kept, `+n earlier`) and runs again inside the same panel. Four illustrative stories cycle: plan the launch, draft the release notes, summarize the week, why the deploy failed.

The field opens empty with the example prompt as its placeholder. Enter on an empty field types the example first. After roughly six and a half seconds with no key or tap, the page types a thought by itself and sends it; any keypress or tap cancels that and clears what the page had typed, and Enter while it is still typing completes the thought rather than sending a fragment. Focus is shown in the field's own underline, not as an outline box. While the organism works, the send arrow becomes a stop square; stopping keeps what was already reached, lets the rest go, and hands the composer back. What the organism reaches for outside the panel are cards (a glyph, a title, a subtitle) held in pockets of membrane: memories fold to their glyph and ride in onto their row, agents receive the task packet, the kept card is carried out; on narrow screens the cards are glyph-only. All six organisms have eyes, and the lead reacts: it widens, half-lids, squints and blinks with the story, and the right head wakes to watch the teammates. Uses the actual Cojeev ShaderBackground, InputControl, Button, AnimatedIcon, BubbleContent, ThemeToggle and Drawer (the teammate initials disc uses the avatar tokens directly, since this app does not install the avatar's Radix dependency), plus Motion sequences on a plain-object world painted by one frame loop. The membrane is a local WebGL prototype in library source (`registry/cojeev/lib/membrane-field.ts`, `registry/cojeev/ui/membrane.tsx`) with a 2D canvas fallback; it is not a registry entry. Countdown shares launch.json; with no published date it runs a preview clock (thirty days from the first visit, kept in the browser) with rolling digits, in the header's right corner. Two layers sit between the shader and everything else: a frost (backdrop blur and a paper tint) and a clearing (a radial pool of paper, ink in dark, under the composition). The shader itself runs at a third of its source speed with a narrow dark palette. The bar is three zones: Replay and Pause left, What's coming centred, theme right.

A local pause stops the story, the typing, the roaming and shader rendering; the open footer and a hidden page do the same. Reduced motion shows every beat as a readable still, with Back and Next for stepping and no auto-start. The dark background sits its camera slightly closer than the source preset so the plane's corners stay outside wide frames. Rows, memories, teammates and automations are illustrative, with no agent integration. The production build has entries for index.html, resident.html and mind.html; the earlier Type-direction home page was removed on 14 September 2026 when the Bond became the home page. Evidence: verification/prompt-bond.md.

## Two additional story demos

`/resident.html`: The Resident inhabits the border of a suggested computer window. Five scenes (Meet, Remember, Connect, Notice, Stay) auto-advance while visible and moving; chapter controls and replay remain available. The Notice scene accepts an illustrative skill suggestion. Stay changes the resident's inner intelligence. No actual skill or backend is created.

`/mind.html`: Changing Mind is a sculptural SVG/CSS volume study, using perspective, membrane layers and Cojeev shapes. It is not a new 3D WebGL scene. Creative/Analytical/Curious controls change the core. Up to three illustrative thought beads persist across mind and character changes within this page instance. Gentle/Playful changes the membrane rhythm independently. Clear resets the beads. There is no durable memory storage.

All three story demos have shared comparison links. The two new demos share theme, pause, countdown, visibility/quiet policy and the curved feature drawer; they reuse the existing shader and add no dependencies. Vite builds the main landing plus all three demo HTML entries. See verification/other-demos.md.
